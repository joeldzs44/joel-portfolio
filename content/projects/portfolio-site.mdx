<Problem label="01 / The problem" title="Public input needs safeguards.">

I wanted visitors to be able to ask about my work in their own words, rather than search through pages for it.

A public AI chat can be used as a free general-purpose LLM, scripted, or steered off-topic, and a gallery or contact form attracts spam. Every feature had to be safe to leave running unattended, on a small budget.

</Problem>

<Approach label="02 / System design" title="Static where possible, guarded where not." intro="Next.js on Vercel, with three small APIs that each check who is calling before doing any work.">

<Stage step="01 / Pages" title="Content as files">
Projects, experience and writing live as typed files in the repository, rendered to static pages. The same files are what the AI assistant reads.
</Stage>

<Stage step="02 / Interview" title="An assistant that stays on topic">
Answers stream from a model on OpenRouter, grounded only in the portfolio content. Off-topic questions get a fixed refusal, and repeated questions are answered from a cache.
</Stage>

<Stage step="03 / Gallery" title="Drawings, moderated">
Visitors draw on a canvas with a handful of brushes. Submissions wait in private storage until I approve them, then move to a public bucket.
</Stage>

<Stage step="04 / Contact" title="Messages without an address">
The contact form sends through Resend, so neither my email address nor my phone number appears anywhere on the site.
</Stage>

<Decision title="Keep the assistant on topic.">
Each session is bound to the visitor's hashed IP, questions are capped in length, and every previous answer the browser sends back must carry a signature from the server. That stops a script from supplying its own conversation history to steer the model, and a Turnstile check plus rate limits keep volume manageable.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Answer repeated questions from a cache.">
Answers are cached against a hash of the portfolio content for up to a week. A popular question costs one model call, and editing the content invalidates the cache on its own.
</Decision>

</Approach>

<Reflection label="03 / Evaluation" title="How it's maintained." subtitle="Small in scope, run like production.">

The site is small, but it follows the practices I use at work: tests for the guard logic, a strict content security policy, secrets kept on the server, and row-level security on every table.

The interview assistant uses the same pattern as much of my professional work: an LLM grounded in a fixed body of knowledge, with guardrails around it.

</Reflection>

<Takeaways>

<Takeaway label="04 / What I’d keep" title="Design for misuse first.">
Planning the interview around misuse first made the rest simple: once the checks were in place, ordinary questions needed almost no special handling.
</Takeaway>
